# VIKO ASHURA

## The King Who Chose to Forget

**VIKO ASHURA** is an interactive character-story experience built for the **SkyCastle Studio AI Hackathon**.

Viko erased the King he used to be. Now the Queen he chose to forget returns with one truth: **humanity may survive only if he becomes that King again.**

He did not just forget a throne.

**He forgot the woman who ruled beside him.**

> Choose once. Accept the consequence.

### 🏆 Grand Prize — Studio AI Hackathon

VIKO ASHURA won the **Grand Prize ($300)** in the SkyCastle Studio AI Hackathon.

The winning build went beyond a character image and cinematic clip by combining AI-generated visual storytelling with a **VibeFlow interactive experience**, allowing the player to make the central decision in Viko's story and live with its consequence.

## Experience

The project is built around a simple question:

**Would you remember?**

Instead of presenting Viko as a linear character profile, the experience turns his internal conflict into an interaction. The player chooses whether Viko embraces the identity he deliberately erased or protects the life he chose afterward.

The choice is intentionally part of the story rather than an extra UI mechanic.

## Character

### Viko Ashura

A former king who deliberately erased the person he once was.

His visual identity is built around a fragmented royal past and a chosen present: dark clothing, aged-gold Ashura details, a broken-crown motif, and the visual language of memory, restraint, and power held back.

### Kleopatra

The Queen Viko chose to forget.

Her return forces the central conflict of the story: the life Viko deliberately chose versus the identity—and responsibility—he erased.

## Core Story Idea

The project started from a contradiction:

**What if the strongest thing a king ever did was erase himself—and the world eventually needed that king back?**

That creates three conflicts at once:

- **Personal:** Viko must confront the self he intentionally destroyed.
- **Relational:** Kleopatra remembers a shared life that Viko no longer does.
- **Global:** remembering may be necessary for humanity to survive.

The interactive format makes the audience responsible for resolving that conflict.
